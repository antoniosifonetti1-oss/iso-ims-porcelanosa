The case

Porcelanosa: export-driven, 7 brands, 200+ stores, already ISO-certified in parts

Hypotheses, not facts. Porcelanosa does not publish its internal set-up. We assume each brand runs its own systems and that there is no group-wide ISO 27001. The conclusion holds even if some certificates already exist: the gain is integration, governance and one owner for AI risk.

Why these numbers: every driver and assumption

Porcelanosa publishes no internal costs, so the set-up cost is a bottom-up estimate (effort × rate). The drivers are our assumptions for a ~5,000-person group with plant (OT) networks, reconciled with the range in our briefing. Order of magnitude, about ±30%. Public ISO 27001 cost guides quote tens of thousands of dollars but for small software firms; they do not scale to a multi-site manufacturer.

Rates used

Consultant ≈ €1,000 a day; internal FTE ≈ €75 k loaded a year; staff time ≈ €25 an hour. Our assumptions for Spain, not quotes.

Audit saving ≈ €50 k a year

Assumed current audit fees for 9001, 14001 and 50001 across the brands ≈ €250 k a year. IAF MD 11 caps integrated-audit reduction at 20% (2019 text): 20% × €250 k = €50 k.

First task of phase 1: replace these assumptions with three quotes per line and the real audit invoices. If the real total is above the high case, the plan stops at gate 1.

        Why integration works: six clause rows are identical, only clause 8 differs

        The four standards in 2026

        Capability vs. hype. A certificate proves that a management system exists and is audited, not that a product or AI model is good. ISO 42001 is not compliance with the EU AI Act: it is a management framework, the law sets legal duties.
